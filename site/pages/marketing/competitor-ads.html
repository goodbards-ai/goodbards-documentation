    Competitor ads¶

    See the ads a competitor is running on Google right now (Search, YouTube, Display, Shopping and Maps), straight from Google's Ads Transparency Center. In the app: Marketing → Digital Ads → Competitor ads.

    What it's for¶

    Before you write an ad, it helps to know what your competitors are saying, which formats they use and which ads they keep paying for. Google publishes every advertiser's ads; this tab puts them in front of you without leaving GoodBards.

    Before you start¶

    Experimental

    Competitor ads is an experimental feature, switched on company by company. If you don't see the Competitor ads tab (marked Experimental), ask GoodBards to switch it on for your company.

    • Nothing to connect. The ads are public, so you don't need a Google Ads account.
    • Your competitors saved. Add them once under Settings → Company → Competitors: each one is listed in the Competitor list at the top of the tab. Until you have some, the tab shows an Add competitors link and a field to type any brand.

    How to look up a competitor¶

    The bar at the top of the tab says what the whole page is about: one competitor, in one country.

    1. In Competitor, choose one of your competitors. Their ads load at once.
    2. In Country, choose where to look. Singapore is selected first, every South-East Asian country is in the list, plus All countries.
    3. For a brand you haven't saved, choose Other brand… at the bottom of the list. In the window that opens, type its website (castlery.com, or paste any page of the site) or its name, then Search.

    Brands you looked up this way are listed under Other searches in the same list, so you can open them again in one click. A green dot means opening it is free (searched in the last 24 hours). To tidy the list, click the ✕ next to a search: it disappears from the list but still counts in this month's searches. Manage competitors at the bottom opens your Competitors page.

    Under the bar, the competitor's name, website and country head the page, and every section below is titled with their name.

    Search the website rather than the name

    A website finds every account advertising that site, whatever the company is called in each country. A brand name only finds advertisers whose name matches.

    How to read the results¶

    The four tiles at the top:

    • Ads shown: the ads in the list (the 100 most recently shown), out of everything Google holds for this competitor.
    • New ads: ads first shown in the last 30 days, so what they have just launched.
    • Longest running: the ad that has been shown the most days.
    • Text · Image · Video: how many ads of each format.

    Then two short rows answer the two questions that matter:

    • What competitor just launched: up to 4 ads first shown in the last 30 days, newest first. This is what the competitor is starting or testing now.
    • What competitor keeps running: the 4 older ads shown for the most days. These are probably working for them.

    Each card shows the ad's picture, the advertiser's legal name, how many days it has run, its first and last day and the website it sends people to. Click a card to open the ad on Google's own page. Video ads play there only.

    Look at who runs the ads

    The advertiser on a card can be an agency rather than the brand itself. That tells you who handles their campaigns.

    Click See all … ads to open the full list. There, show only Text, Image or Video ads, sort by Longest running or Newest, and, when a competitor advertises from several companies (one per country, say), pick one advertiser.

    Longest running is a hint, not a result

    Google shares no spend, clicks or sales for these ads. An ad that has run for months tells you the advertiser kept paying for it, which usually means it works, but it isn't proof. A short promotion can perform better and still stop after two weeks.

    Your monthly searches¶

    Each search uses one of your company's monthly searches. The line under the search field shows how many are left and when they start again (on the 1st of each month).

    • Opening the same competitor and country again within 24 hours is free: the result is kept, and its label says Saved result, no search used. Your recent searches appear as buttons; a green dot means they are still free.
    • Refresh fetches the ads again from Google and uses one search.
    • A search that finds no ads uses nothing.

    When competitor ads are unavailable

    If the red message Competitor ads are unavailable appears, the service behind this tab has used its allowance for the whole platform, not just yours. It comes back when it renews. Contact your administrator.

    Good to know¶

    • The list holds the 100 most recently shown ads. A large advertiser can have thousands. Narrow it down by choosing a country.
    • Ad wording isn't extracted as text. Text ads appear as a picture of the ad, just as Google shows them.
    • Competitor ads works for Google only. Meta (Facebook, Instagram) has its own public Ad Library.
