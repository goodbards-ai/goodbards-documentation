    Automations¶

    Send each contact a sequence of emails, waits and follow-ups that runs on its own once you switch it on.

    What it's for¶

    A flow is a path one contact walks through: a welcome email, a wait of three days, a second email only for those who opened the first, a tag at the end. You design it once; GoodBards then runs it separately for every contact who enters, at their own pace.

    Use it for welcome sequences after a sign-up form, nurture series for new leads, re-engagement of quiet contacts, or a monthly touchpoint for a segment.

    Before you start¶

    • Your plan. Automations is part of the Pro and Team plans. If Automations shows a crown in the sidebar, your plan does not include it; speak to your account contact.
    • Your access. Flows sit with emails, so you need the Emails and forms access (write, to build and activate flows). See Users, roles & permissions.
    • An email server. Flows send through the server connected under Connections. See Email sending.
    • The emails themselves. Each email in a flow is one of your Emails. Create them first, or add the flow's steps and pick the emails afterwards.
    • Subscribed contacts. Only contacts whose status is Subscribed stay in a flow (see Good to know).

    How to build a flow¶

    1. In the sidebar, choose Marketing → Automations, then New Flow.

    2. Give it a Name and, if you like, a Description, then choose Create. The designer opens.

    3. Select the Trigger card at the top and pick what enrols a contact under Starts when (see Triggers). Fill in the form, tag or segment it asks for.

    4. Add steps. Choose the + under a card: Add a step lists every step with what it does, and the one you pick goes where you clicked. You can also drag a step from the palette on the left onto a +, or use Edit → Add step.

    5. Select each card and fill in its settings in the side panel on the right: the email to send, how long to wait, the tag to add.

    6. Open File → Flow settings to choose the re-entry rule, a goal and any Stop early when… rules (see Flow settings).

    7. Choose Save. An unfinished flow can be saved; the cards still missing something are highlighted.

    8. When every card is complete, choose Activate. New contacts enrol from that moment.

    Test it on a few people first

    Once the flow is active, open its Contacts tab and choose Enroll contacts to run it on yourself or a couple of colleagues before the real audience arrives. (On a Manual flow, the same button sits on the Trigger card in the designer.)

    Other ways to start¶

    • Duplicate an existing flow from its menu in the list.
    • AI assistant: in the designer, switch the view to AI assistant, paste a brief or describe the flow in your own words. It asks up to five questions, then proposes a flow. Choose Apply to the canvas, review it, then Save. It also lists the emails you still need to create.
    • Import JSON…: load a flow someone exported with Export as JSON, for example from another workspace.

    Triggers¶

    The trigger decides who enters the flow.

    Nothing is backfilled

    Only events after you activate the flow count: earlier form submissions, tags or contacts are never picked up. To include people who filled in the form last week, enrol them yourself.

    Trigger Who enters
    Manual Only the contacts you enrol yourself, by hand, from a segment or from a mailing list.
    Form submitted The contact of every new submission of the chosen form, within a minute.
    Contact created Every contact added after activation, within a minute.
    Tag added A contact who gets the chosen tag, within a minute, whoever adds it.
    Segment (daily) Every day at 03:00 UTC, members of the segment who are not in the flow yet.
    On a schedule Members of the segment, each time the schedule runs: every day, week or month at a time and timezone you choose.

    Whatever the trigger, you can also enrol contacts yourself while the flow is active: on the flow's Contacts tab, choose Enroll contacts and pick Contacts, a Segment or a Mailing list (up to 2,000 at a time).

    Steps¶

    Step What it does
    Send email Sends one of your emails, as it is at send time, with the contact's details, tracking and the unsubscribe link.
    Wait Pauses for a number of minutes, hours or days (up to 365 days).
    Wait for open / click Pauses until the contact opens or clicks an earlier email of the flow, or a time limit passes.
    Add tag Puts a tag on the contact.
    Remove tag Takes a tag off the contact.
    Set field Writes a value into one of the contact's custom fields.
    Ask an agent Asks one of your AI agents about the contact. Its answer, free text or a drafted email, is kept for the next steps.
    Task for the owner Puts a task in Actions for the contact's owner or a named colleague. The flow waits until it is done, dismissed or past its deadline.
    Send drafted email Sends the email an Ask an agent step drafted, inside one of your email designs.

    The palette's Structure group splits contacts into lanes:

    Step What it does
    Yes / No branch Two lanes: contacts who match a condition, and everyone else.
    Routing Several lanes, one per rule, checked in order, plus a lane for everyone else.
    AI decides The AI answers one question about the contact (for example Is this lead hot, warm or cold?) and sends them down the matching lane. When it is not sure enough, they take the last lane.
    Exit flow The contact leaves the flow here.

    Conditions in branches, routes, goals and exit rules can check a tag, a field value, membership of a segment, whether the contact opened or clicked an earlier email, the outcome of a task, or what an earlier step wrote.

    Under Decoration, Add note puts a sticky note on the canvas for your colleagues. Contacts never see it.

    Flow settings¶

    In the designer, choose File → Flow settings.

    Setting What it decides
    Send emails with Which email server sends, when you have more than one connected.
    Re-entry Once per contact (the default); Every time the trigger fires again (after finishing); or After a cooldown, with the number of days before the same contact can enter again.
    AI budget per month The most credits this flow may spend on Ask an agent and AI decides steps each calendar month. Empty means no cap.
    Goal What the flow is for, for example Booked a demo. A contact who meets it leaves the flow and counts as a conversion in the report.
    Stop early when… Rules that take a contact out as soon as something becomes true, such as a purchase. A rule can also move them into another flow.

    Running a flow¶

    A flow's status shows in the list:

    Status What it means
    Draft Being built. Nobody enters.
    Active Running. New contacts enrol as the trigger fires.
    Paused Contacts hold where they are, and nobody new enrols until you choose Resume.
    Archived Finished for good. Everyone still in it leaves. It can be viewed but not edited.

    Changing an active flow

    Edits go into a draft; the flow keeps running as it was. Choose Activate changes to publish them. Contacts already in the flow finish on the version they started; new contacts get the new one.

    Following the results¶

    From the list, choose a flow's menu, then Contacts & report.

    • Contacts lists everyone who entered, with their status, the step they are on and why they left. Filter by In flow, Completed, Exited, Skipped or Failed. Path shows the cards a contact went through, with the time of each. Wake now ends a contact's current wait early; Remove takes them out of the flow.
    • Report draws the flow with how many contacts reached each card, the sent, opened and clicked figures of each email, and how lanes split. With a goal, it also shows Goal reached and the Conversion rate.

    A contact's own page also lists the flows they have been in, under Automation flows.

    Using a flow in a campaign¶

    In a campaign, add a Marketing Automation tactic. In the tactic, choose Create the flow, or pick one you already have and choose Link. The tactic then shows how many contacts are In the flow, Completed and Exited, and the flow's emails reach the campaign report.

    The tactic follows the flow: activating the flow starts the tactic, archiving it completes the tactic. See Campaigns.

    Good to know¶

    Only subscribed contacts stay in a flow

    A contact who is not Subscribed (regular, unsubscribed, bounced, do not contact) leaves every flow within a minute, wherever they are, even if the flow sends no email. An unsubscribe from any email therefore ends their flows.

    • One flow at a time. A contact already in a flow is skipped by any other flow's trigger. The skip is listed on that flow's Contacts tab under Skipped, so you can see who missed out.
    • Goals and exit rules are checked before each step, not during a wait. A contact on a three-day wait who books a demo on day one leaves when the wait ends, before the next email goes out.
    • Emails are sent as they are at send time. Editing an email changes what the next contacts receive, even mid-flow.
    • A failing step is retried twice. After the third failure, for example with no email server connected, the contact is marked Failed and leaves. The same happens to Ask an agent and AI decides steps once the flow's monthly AI budget is spent.
    • A flow has at most 50 steps.

    Review AI-drafted emails

    Put a Task for the owner before a Send drafted email step, so a colleague approves or edits the draft in Actions before it goes out.

    Archiving cannot be undone

    You can only delete a draft, or an archived flow that never had a contact. Otherwise, archive it. Everyone still in an archived flow leaves it.
